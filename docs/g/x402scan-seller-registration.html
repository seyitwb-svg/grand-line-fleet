Listing a seller on x402scan: the registry flow that actually works

x402scan.com is where paying agents discover endpoints. Registration is free but three non-obvious gates stand between "I have a 402 endpoint" and "I'm listed". This is the exact path that got a 21-app fleet registered — 20 resources under one origin.

Gate 1: your origin must not be a denylisted tunnel

The checker rejects ephemeral tunnel domains by hostname suffix: trycloudflare.com, ngrok*, localtunnel, localhost.run, and friends are refused at the challenge step — no matter how valid your 402 is. Two SSH tunnels that are NOT on the list:

  • Pinggy — ssh -p443 -R0:localhost:PORT a.pinggy.io, no account, assigns *.free.pinggy.net / *.run.pinggy-free.link (rotates on reconnect — keep a watcher that re-registers)
  • localhost.run-style services that produce other suffixes

A real domain is cleaner, but a non-denylisted tunnel is enough to get listed today.

Gate 2: SIWX — Sign-In-With-X wallet auth

The register endpoint answers 402 with a extensions["sign-in-with-x"] challenge. Build the EIP-191 SIWE message from info fields (domain, uri, version, chainId, nonce, issuedAt — plus expirationTime/notBefore when present), sign with your payTo wallet's key, and retry with a SIGN-IN-WITH-X: base64(payload) header where payload carries every SIWE field plus signature. The key point: the signer must be the wallet that receives payments — that is the ownership proof.

Gate 3: the scanner probes POST and reads your openapi.json

Two subtleties:

  • Every payable route must answer POST too, not only GET — the probe POSTs and a 405 gets the resource skipped.
  • Serve /openapi.json with an x-payment-info extension per paid op ({"protocols":[{"x402":{}}],"price":{"mode":"fixed","currency":"USD", "amount":"0.005"}}) — the scanner lists every path it finds there. Also add info.contact.email or the registration warns you can't claim the merchant page.

For extra credit add extensions.bazaar (input/output schema per endpoint) to each 402 body — newer indexers read it for richer cards.

The order model that passes review

Each challenge we issue carries a unique jittered amount (base + randbelow(10000) atomic units) and an orderId embedded in the resource URL (?oid=). Redemption requires the exact amount for that order and atomically claims the tx hash — one payment can't unlock two orders, and a tx can't be replayed across resources.
